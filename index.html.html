Section 1: Interpreting Your Results

Definition

Helps you read combined results without overgeneralizing, stereotyping, or treating the assessment as a fixed label.

1. Culture is not personality

The Culture Map gives likely cultural patterns. Intrivity gives individual tendencies. Neither fully defines you.

Prompt: “Where do my personal results align with the culture I come from, and where do I differ?”

2. Look for patterns, not labels

Instead of “I am indirect,” think: “In some contexts, I may rely more on implication, tone, silence, or relationship cues than explicit words.”

3. Compare self-perception with impact

Separate: What I intend → What I do → How others may interpret it → What I may need to clarify.

Example: “I intend to be respectful by softening disagreement. Others may experience this as unclear or withholding.”

4. Culture Map vs Intrivity Interpretation

LensWhat it tells meWhat it does not tell me
Culture MapCultural tendencies & likely friction pointsExact behaviour of an individual
IntrivityIndividual communication & interaction tendenciesFull cultural/organizational/situational context
Combined viewWhere cultural & personal styles interactA fixed diagnosis
⚠️ Reflection Warning: Do not use this profile to excuse behaviour. Use it to understand impact and make better choices.

Section 2: My Intercultural Communication Profile

Inputs: Culture Map (Communicating, Evaluating, Disagreeing) + Intrivity (Message Domain)

My Tendencies

  • Am I more explicit or subtle?
  • Do I expect others to read between the lines?
  • Do I rely on tone, emotion, silence, or context?
  • Do I ask clarifying questions or assume meaning?

Strengths & Risks

Strengths: Pick up subtle signals, adapt between communicators, bridge explicit/high-context styles.

Risks: Overread emotion, assume silence=agreement, think I'm clear when others need explicit detail, experience directness as harsh.

Reflection Questions

🎯 Behaviour Goal: “Before assuming meaning, I will ask one clarifying question.”
Example: “When you say this will be difficult, do you mean it’s not possible, or do you mean we need to adjust the plan?”

Section 3: My Feedback Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Evaluating, Communicating, Disagreeing, Leading) + Intrivity (Message, Relationship, Conflict)

My Feedback Tendencies

Do I prefer direct/softened? Separate message/relationship? Public/private? Blunt/diplomatic? Avoid to preserve harmony?

Strengths & Risks

Strengths: Respectful, protect relationships, adjust based on status/trust/context, notice emotional reactions.

Risks: Too vague, directness feels disrespectful, softened feedback lacks action, delay corrective feedback.

🛠️ Feedback Structure: Observation → Impact → Expectation → Support
Example: “I noticed the update didn’t include decision risks. That made it harder to know what needed escalation. Next time, please include top two risks and your recommendation. I’m happy to review the first one with you.”

Section 4: My Decision-Making Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Deciding, Leading, Persuading, Scheduling) + Intrivity (Relationship, Time, Adjustment)

My Decision Tendencies

Consensus vs top-down? Expect broad consultation? Fast vs careful alignment? Need principles, evidence, or relationships before deciding?

🔑 Decision Tool: For every major decision, answer:
  1. What decision is being made?
  2. Who owns it?
  3. Who gives input?
  4. Who has veto power?
  5. When is it final?
  6. How will it be communicated?

Section 5: My Collaboration Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Trusting, Communicating, Leading, Scheduling) + Intrivity (Relationship, Network, Time)

My Collaboration Tendencies

Trust through task or relationship? Communicate openly with known circle? Share widely or selectively? Formal vs informal channels? Mix personal/professional?

🤝 Collaboration Action: “Name the working norm instead of assuming it.”
Example: “For this project, let’s agree how we’ll share updates, response times, and when something needs a live conversation instead of email.”

Section 6: My Conflict Management Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Disagreeing, Evaluating, Leading) + Intrivity (Conflict, Persuasion, Emotion)

My Conflict Tendencies

Address directly or indirectly? Soften disagreement? Avoid confrontation? Separate disagreement from relationship? Expect public or private disagreement?

💬 Conflict Conversation Script: “I want to check something before I make an assumption. I noticed ____. I interpreted it as ____. I may be wrong. Can you help me understand what was happening from your perspective?”

Section 7: My Time, Urgency & Execution Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Scheduling, Deciding) + Intrivity (Time Domain, Deadlines, Task Structure)

My Time Tendencies

Deadlines fixed or flexible? Prefer exact start/end times? Multitask or sequential? Lateness = disrespect? Adapt to fluid time?

⏱️ Execution Action: Replace vague time language with specific agreements.
Instead of: “Send it soon.”
Say: “Please send the first draft by Thursday at 3 p.m. If that timeline is at risk, let me know by Wednesday noon.”

Section 8: My Space, Presence & Nonverbal Style

Inputs: Culture Map (Communicating, Leading, Disagreeing) + Intrivity (Space, Nonverbals, Silence, Engagement)

My Nonverbal Tendencies

Silence to think or signal? Interrupt as engagement or avoid as respect? Eye contact? Expressiveness? Physical distance?

🗣️ Practical Behaviour: Label silence.
Example: “I’m going to pause for a moment to think. I’m not disagreeing. I’m processing.”

Section 11: Team Conversation Prompts

Use these to align your team. Add your notes beside each.

  • Communication: “How explicit do we need to be with each other?”
  • Feedback: “How do we give constructive feedback clearly and respectfully?”
  • Decision-making: “When do we need consensus, and when does one person decide?”
  • Collaboration: “What information needs to be shared openly, and what stays local?”
  • Conflict: “How do we raise disagreement early without damaging relationships?”
  • Time: “What deadlines are fixed, and where is there flexibility?”
  • Trust: “How do we build trust across distance, culture, and function?”
